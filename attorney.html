Plan ahead,
while you still can.

A Lasting Power of Attorney appoints someone you trust to make decisions on your behalf if you are ever unable to — through illness, an accident or in later life. It can only be made while you have the mental capacity to do so, which is why we always recommend putting one in place sooner rather than later.

Why it matters

Without an LPA, your family may have to ask the Court.

Many people assume that a spouse or close relative can automatically step in and manage their affairs if they lose capacity. In fact, without a valid LPA, nobody — not even a husband, wife or child — has an automatic right to do so. Your family would instead need to apply to the Court of Protection to be appointed as a deputy, a process that is slower, more expensive and more intrusive than making an LPA in advance.

An LPA can only be made by someone who has the mental capacity to understand what they are signing. That is why we encourage clients to put one in place well before it might ever be needed — alongside a Will, as part of sensible planning for the future.

  • Appoint people you already know and trust
  • Covers property & financial decisions, health & welfare decisions, or both
  • Must be registered with the Office of the Public Guardian before use
  • Avoids a costly, time-consuming Court of Protection application
What we handle

Two types of Lasting Power of Attorney.

01

Property & Financial Affairs LPA

This LPA allows your chosen attorneys to manage your bank accounts, pay your bills, deal with your property and handle your investments and financial affairs generally. With your permission, it can be used even while you still have capacity — for example, if you find it difficult to get to the bank — as well as if you later lose capacity.

  • Managing bank and savings accounts
  • Paying bills and managing income and expenditure
  • Buying, selling or renting out property
  • Managing investments and pensions
  • Dealing with tax affairs and benefits
02

Health & Welfare LPA

This LPA allows your chosen attorneys to make decisions about your daily routine, medical care and, if you wish, life-sustaining treatment — but only once you have lost the capacity to make those decisions yourself. It gives your attorneys the authority to speak with doctors and care providers on your behalf, and to make sure your wishes are respected.

  • Decisions about daily routine and where you live
  • Medical care and treatment decisions
  • Life-sustaining treatment (only if you specifically choose to include this)
  • Liaising with doctors, care homes and social services
03

LPAs for Business Owners

If you own or run a business, a personal LPA alone may not be enough — it does not automatically give your attorney the power to make decisions about your business interests. We advise business owners on a separate, business-specific LPA, so that your company or partnership can continue to operate smoothly if you are ever unable to be involved.

  • Business LPAs alongside your personal LPA
  • Advice for sole traders, partners and company directors
  • Coordinating with shareholders' and partnership agreements
04

Registration & Ongoing Duties

An LPA must be registered with the Office of the Public Guardian before it can be used. We prepare your LPA correctly from the outset, manage the registration process on your behalf, and are on hand to advise your attorneys on their duties and responsibilities once appointed.

  • Preparation and execution of your LPA documents
  • Registration with the Office of the Public Guardian
  • Advice for attorneys on their legal duties
  • Safe storage of your registered LPA

you can.
An LPA cannot be made after capacity is lost
Without an LPA

The Court of Protection route is slower and more costly.

If you lose capacity without an LPA in place, a family member or friend must apply to the Court of Protection to be appointed as a deputy before they can manage your affairs. This process typically takes several months, involves court fees, and requires the deputy to report to the Court on an ongoing basis — a burden an LPA avoids entirely.

Putting an LPA in place now, while you have capacity, is one of the simplest and most valuable pieces of planning you can do for yourself and the people who care for you.
