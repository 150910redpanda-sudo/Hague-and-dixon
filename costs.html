How we charge

You may choose to instruct us to work by reference to the hourly rates below (+ VAT) applicable to the lawyer or lawyers who undertake your work. The advantage is that you would only pay for the time you use. The disadvantage is that you would not have an estimate for your budget. If you prefer, you can ask us to provide you with an estimate and we would gladly oblige. For residential conveyancing and probate and administration of estates work, we will provide you with an estimate at the outset in any event. If your work is more complex than originally envisaged, or if additional work not in scope of the original estimate is required then we will review the orignial estimate with you at the earliest opportunity. We will send you our letter of engagement at the outset. It will, amoungst other things, confirm any verbal estimate of costs in writing. This letter is our invitation to you to instruct us. Your obligation to us begins when you confirm your instructions by replying to this letter.

All fees shown are exclusive of VAT (currently 20%). Disbursements are payments made on your behalf to third parties such as the Land Registry or HM Courts and Tribunals Service — these are in addition to our legal fees.

Hourly rates

Where work is charged by time rather than at a fixed fee.

For matters such as family law, business and commercial work, Court of Protection applications, and any complex or contentious matters, our charges are based on time spent at the following hourly rates. These rates are reviewed periodically.

The weeks shown are indicative for a straightforward transaction and assume the other parties in the chain move at the same pace. Our fee assumes the transaction is concluded in a timely manner and that no unforeseen complications arise — for example a defect in title, a missing building regulation consent, or the need for an indemnity policy or a lease extension. If any of these arise we will tell you promptly what it means for both the cost and the timescale, before we do the additional work.

How long does it take? A straightforward freehold purchase or sale typically takes 8–10 weeks from instruction. Leasehold, new-build or chain transactions commonly take 12–16 weeks, and occasionally longer where a management company is slow to provide information or a lease needs extending. We will keep you informed throughout.

Estate Administration (Probate)

Uncontested estate administration. More about wills & probate →

The cost of administering an estate depends on its complexity — the number of assets, whether inheritance tax applies, and whether there is a valid Will. We charge on a time-spent basis using our hourly rates. The indicative ranges below are for straightforward, uncontested estates. All fees are exclusive of VAT at 20%.

Probate application fees are set by HMCTS and are subject to change. Inheritance tax payable to HMRC is in addition and depends on the size and composition of the estate.

Key stages

1. Taking your instructions, identifying the executors or administrators, and establishing whether there is a valid WillWeeks 1–2
2. Identifying and valuing the assets and liabilities of the estate, writing to banks, insurers and other institutionsWeeks 2–10
3. Preparing the inheritance tax account and the Legal Statement, and arranging payment of any tax dueMonths 2–4
4. Applying to the Probate Registry for the Grant of Probate or Letters of AdministrationMonths 3–5
5. Receiving the Grant and collecting in the assets of the estateMonths 4–6
6. Settling liabilities, and placing statutory notices where you instruct us toMonths 4–6
7. Preparing estate accounts and distributing to the beneficiariesMonths 5–7

Timings depend heavily on matters outside our control — principally how quickly third parties respond and current HMRC and Probate Registry processing times. Where inheritance tax is payable, the Grant cannot be applied for until HMRC has processed the account, which commonly adds several weeks. Statutory notices, if placed, require a two-month waiting period before it is safe to distribute.

What does this include? Taking your initial instructions, advising on the estate, preparing the application for the Grant of Probate (or Letters of Administration), corresponding with banks, HMRC and other institutions, collecting and distributing the estate, and providing a full estate account to all beneficiaries. A straightforward estate typically takes 3–7 months to conclude.

What this does not include. Selling or transferring any property in the estate is charged separately at our conveyancing rates. Specific tax advice, the preparation of trusts arising under the Will, dealing with overseas assets, and defending a claim against the estate all fall outside these figures and would be quoted separately.

We do not offer conditional fee or damages-based agreements for any of our services, including Employment Tribunal claims. If your matter is one where such an arrangement might suit you better, we will say so and you are free to instruct a firm that offers one.

Other ways your costs may be met

  • Existing insurance — many home, motor and travel policies, and some credit cards and trade union memberships, include legal expenses cover. It is well worth checking before you instruct us, and we will ask you about it at the outset.
  • Your employer — where you have been offered a settlement agreement, the employer will usually contribute to or meet the whole of your legal fee for the advice the law requires you to take.

If you are eligible for legal aid, you can find a firm that holds a contract through the GOV.UK legal adviser search. We would rather tell you this at the start than after you have paid us.

— Important notes

VAT & Disbursements

Our legal fees are subject to VAT at the current standard rate of 20%. Disbursements are payments we make to third parties on your behalf — they are not our fees and are passed on at cost. Examples include:

  • Land Registry fees — set by HM Land Registry on a published scale
  • Search fees — payable to local authorities and search providers
  • Probate application fee — payable to HM Courts & Tribunals Service
  • Stamp Duty Land Tax — a government tax on property transactions; we will calculate the amount due and submit the return on your behalf
  • ID verification and AML checks — as required by the Money Laundering Regulations

We will itemise all anticipated disbursements in our initial estimate, so there are no surprises. If any additional costs arise, we will notify you promptly before incurring them.
