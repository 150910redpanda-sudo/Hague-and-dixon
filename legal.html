The SRA sets the standards that all law firms in England and Wales must meet. You can check our authorisation, and the practising status of any of our solicitors, free of charge on the SRA's public register. The professional rules we are bound by — the SRA Standards and Regulations — are published at sra.org.uk.

Professional indemnity insurance

We hold compulsory professional indemnity insurance that meets the SRA's Minimum Terms and Conditions. The territorial coverage of that insurance is England and Wales, and it applies to the legal services we provide from each of our three offices. The name and contact details of our current insurer, and our policy number, are available on request from any office.

Money we hold for you

Any money we hold on your behalf is held in a client account, separately from the firm's own money, in accordance with the SRA Accounts Rules. We will account to you for a fair sum of interest on money we hold for you where it is fair and reasonable to do so; our full interest policy forms part of the Terms of Business sent to you at the start of your matter, and a copy is available on request.

Client money is held at a bank or building society covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS). The FSCS limit applies per person per banking institution, and it applies to your own money held at the same institution as well as to money we hold for you.

Complaints

Our Complaints Procedure

We are committed to providing a high-quality legal service. If something goes wrong or you are unhappy with any aspect of the service you have received, we want to hear about it — it helps us improve.

Step 1 — Raise the matter with us

In the first instance, please raise your concerns with the solicitor handling your matter, or with the person responsible for client care at the firm. You can do this by telephone, email or in writing:

Step 2 — Formal complaint

If the matter is not resolved to your satisfaction, you may make a formal complaint in writing, addressed to the Senior Director at the York office address above. We will acknowledge your complaint within 3 business days and aim to provide a full written response within 8 weeks.

Step 3 — Legal Ombudsman

If you remain dissatisfied following our response, you may refer your complaint to the Legal Ombudsman, which investigates complaints about regulated legal services providers. Their service is free to you.

You must first have complained to us and either received our final written response, or waited eight weeks from the date you complained without receiving one. You must then refer the matter to the Legal Ombudsman:

  • within six months of receiving our final written response; and
  • no later than one year from the date of the act or omission complained of, or one year from the date you should reasonably have realised there was cause for complaint.

The Legal Ombudsman can decide to extend these time limits in exceptional circumstances. Not every client is eligible to use the scheme — it is open to individuals and to smaller businesses, charities and trusts; the Legal Ombudsman will confirm whether it can consider your complaint.

If your complaint is about our bill

You have the right to object to a bill and to apply to the court for it to be assessed under Part III of the Solicitors Act 1974. There are strict time limits for doing so — generally one month from delivery of the bill for the most favourable terms, and the court's power to order an assessment is lost altogether after twelve months.

Please note that if you apply to the court to have a bill assessed, the Legal Ombudsman may decline to consider a complaint about that same bill. If all or part of a bill remains unpaid, we may be entitled to charge interest on the outstanding amount.

SRA — conduct issues

If your complaint relates to a breach of our regulatory obligations — for example concerns about dishonesty, taking or losing your money, or treating you unfairly because of your protected characteristics — you may also report this to the Solicitors Regulation Authority.

ADR

Alternative Dispute Resolution

We are required to inform you of the existence of approved Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) bodies able to deal with complaints about legal services. Certified providers such as ProMediate and Small Claims Mediation exist, but their use is voluntary and requires both parties to agree. We have elected not to use ADR for resolving complaints, other than through the Legal Ombudsman scheme described above.

The EU Online Dispute Resolution platform ceased to operate on 20 July 2025 and no longer accepts complaints. There is no equivalent replacement, and it has not applied to UK legal service providers since the UK left the EU.

Privacy

Privacy & Data Protection

Hague & Dixon LLP is committed to protecting your personal data. We are registered with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) as a data controller. This summary explains the key points of how we handle your personal information.

What data we collect

We collect personal data that you provide to us directly — such as your name, address, contact details, and information relevant to the legal matter on which you are instructing us. We may also collect data from third parties in the course of carrying out legal services (for example, from other solicitors, HMRC or HM Land Registry).

How we use it

We use your personal data to provide legal services, to comply with our regulatory and legal obligations (including Anti-Money Laundering requirements), to manage our relationship with you as a client, and to contact you about matters relevant to your instructions or to our services generally where you have not opted out.

We will not sell your data or share it with third parties for marketing purposes without your consent. We may share your data with third parties where necessary to deliver our services — such as the Land Registry, HMRC, Courts, or other professional advisers — or where required by law.

Retention

We retain client files and associated data for a period of at least six years following the conclusion of a matter, in accordance with our professional obligations and limitation periods. Some data (such as Wills and title deeds) may be retained indefinitely.

Your rights

You have the right to access your personal data, request correction of inaccurate data, and in certain circumstances to request erasure or restriction of processing. To exercise any of these rights, or for a full copy of our Privacy Policy, please contact us at any of our offices.

Terms

Terms of Business

When you instruct us, we will send you our Client Care Letter together with our Terms of Business. These set out the basis on which we will provide our services, including our charges, billing arrangements and the responsibilities of both parties. Please read them carefully.

Our standard terms include the following key points:

  • Fees: As set out in your Client Care Letter or our costs page. All fees are subject to VAT.
  • Billing: We may render interim bills during the course of a matter. Final bills are due within 30 days of issue.
  • Lien: We may retain documents and money held on your behalf if fees remain unpaid.
  • Confidentiality: We keep your affairs confidential, subject to our regulatory and legal obligations.
  • Anti-Money Laundering: We are required by law to verify the identity of all new clients and to carry out checks on the source of funds in certain transactions.
  • Limitation of liability: Our aggregate liability to you in respect of any matter is limited to the minimum level of professional indemnity insurance cover we are required to maintain.

Full terms are provided in writing at the commencement of every matter. If you have any questions about our terms, please ask your solicitor.
