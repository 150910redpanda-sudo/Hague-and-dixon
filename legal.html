Complaints

Our Complaints Procedure

We are committed to providing a high-quality legal service. If something goes wrong or you are unhappy with any aspect of the service you have received, we want to hear about it — it helps us improve.

Step 1 — Raise the matter with us

In the first instance, please raise your concerns with the solicitor handling your matter, or with the person responsible for client care at the firm. You can do this by telephone, email or in writing:

Step 2 — Formal complaint

If the matter is not resolved to your satisfaction, you may make a formal complaint in writing, addressed to the Senior Director at the York office address above. We will acknowledge your complaint within 3 business days and aim to provide a full written response within 8 weeks.

Step 3 — Legal Ombudsman

If you remain dissatisfied following our response, you may refer your complaint to the Legal Ombudsman, which investigates complaints about regulated legal services providers. The Legal Ombudsman expects complaints to be made to them within one year from the date of the act or omission complained of, or within one year of you realising there was a problem. You should also refer to the Ombudsman within six months of receiving a final response from us.

ADR

Alternative Dispute Resolution

We are required by law to inform you of the existence of approved Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) bodies able to deal with complaints about legal services. We have elected not to use ADR for resolving complaints, other than through the Legal Ombudsman scheme described above.

For online purchases or contracts entered into online, you may also refer disputes to the EU Online Dispute Resolution Platform at ec.europa.eu/odr.

Privacy

Privacy & Data Protection

Hague & Dixon LLP is committed to protecting your personal data. We are registered with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) as a data controller. This summary explains the key points of how we handle your personal information.

What data we collect

We collect personal data that you provide to us directly — such as your name, address, contact details, and information relevant to the legal matter on which you are instructing us. We may also collect data from third parties in the course of carrying out legal services (for example, from other solicitors, HMRC or HM Land Registry).

How we use it

We use your personal data to provide legal services, to comply with our regulatory and legal obligations (including Anti-Money Laundering requirements), to manage our relationship with you as a client, and to contact you about matters relevant to your instructions or to our services generally where you have not opted out.

We will not sell your data or share it with third parties for marketing purposes without your consent. We may share your data with third parties where necessary to deliver our services — such as the Land Registry, HMRC, Courts, or other professional advisers — or where required by law.

Retention

We retain client files and associated data for a period of at least six years following the conclusion of a matter, in accordance with our professional obligations and limitation periods. Some data (such as Wills and title deeds) may be retained indefinitely.

Your rights

You have the right to access your personal data, request correction of inaccurate data, and in certain circumstances to request erasure or restriction of processing. To exercise any of these rights, or for a full copy of our Privacy Policy, please contact us at any of our offices.

Terms

Terms of Business

When you instruct us, we will send you our Client Care Letter together with our Terms of Business. These set out the basis on which we will provide our services, including our charges, billing arrangements and the responsibilities of both parties. Please read them carefully.

Our standard terms include the following key points:

  • Fees: As set out in your Client Care Letter or our costs page. All fees are subject to VAT.
  • Billing: We may render interim bills during the course of a matter. Final bills are due within 30 days of issue.
  • Lien: We may retain documents and money held on your behalf if fees remain unpaid.
  • Confidentiality: We keep your affairs confidential, subject to our regulatory and legal obligations.
  • Anti-Money Laundering: We are required by law to verify the identity of all new clients and to carry out checks on the source of funds in certain transactions.
  • Limitation of liability: Our aggregate liability to you in respect of any matter is limited to the minimum level of professional indemnity insurance cover we are required to maintain.

Full terms are provided in writing at the commencement of every matter. If you have any questions about our terms, please ask your solicitor.
