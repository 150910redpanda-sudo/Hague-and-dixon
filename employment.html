Both sides of the table.

Employment law affects everyone in the workplace. We advise both employees facing difficult situations — dismissal, redundancy, discrimination, a settlement offer — and employers navigating the legal responsibilities that come with managing people. Our approach on both sides is the same: clear, practical advice, given promptly.

Many employment matters are time-sensitive. The time limit for bringing an Employment Tribunal claim is usually three months less one day from the act complained of. If you are in any doubt about your situation, contact us as soon as possible.

For employees

Protecting your position at work.

01

Settlement Agreements

A settlement agreement (formerly called a compromise agreement) is a legally binding contract between you and your employer, under which you agree to waive your rights to bring Employment Tribunal claims in exchange for a financial payment. The law requires you to take independent legal advice before signing — and your employer is usually required to contribute to the cost of that advice.

We review the agreement, explain what you are giving up, and advise you on whether the terms are reasonable given your circumstances. In many cases, we can negotiate an improved offer. See our costs page for typical fees.

  • Review and advice on settlement agreement terms
  • Advice on whether the offer is fair
  • Negotiation of improved terms where appropriate
  • Explanation of post-employment restrictions (garden leave, non-competes)
  • Tax advice on the structure of the payment
02

Unfair Dismissal & Wrongful Dismissal

If you have been dismissed and believe the dismissal was unfair or in breach of your contract, you may have a claim against your employer. Unfair dismissal claims must usually be brought within three months less one day of the dismissal date — so it is essential to take advice promptly.

  • Assessment of whether you have a viable claim
  • Advice on the ACAS Early Conciliation process (required before a Tribunal claim)
  • Drafting and submitting Employment Tribunal claim forms
  • Preparation for and representation at Tribunal hearings
  • Claims for notice pay and other contractual entitlements
03

Redundancy

If you are at risk of redundancy, understanding your rights is vital. This includes your right to a fair selection process, a meaningful consultation, statutory redundancy pay, and any enhanced payment your employer may be obliged to make. We advise on whether the process being followed is lawful and what your options are.

  • Advice on the redundancy process and your rights
  • Statutory and enhanced redundancy pay calculations
  • Challenges to unfair selection for redundancy
  • Review of any settlement offer made alongside redundancy
  • TUPE — protection when your employer is taken over
04

Discrimination & Harassment

The Equality Act 2010 protects employees from discrimination on the grounds of protected characteristics including age, disability, sex, race, religion or belief, sexual orientation, pregnancy and maternity, and gender reassignment. If you have been treated unfairly at work for reasons related to any of these characteristics, we can advise you on your options.

  • Advice on direct and indirect discrimination
  • Harassment and victimisation claims
  • Disability discrimination and reasonable adjustments
  • Equal pay claims
  • Internal grievance advice
For employers

Managing your people, managing your risk.

05

Employment Contracts & Policies

A well-drafted employment contract and a clear set of workplace policies are your best defence against employment disputes. We draft contracts of employment, handbooks, and policies tailored to your business — and review existing documents to identify gaps or risks.

  • Contracts of employment (all levels)
  • Director's service agreements
  • Employee handbooks and HR policies
  • Disciplinary and grievance procedures
  • Confidentiality agreements
  • Post-termination restrictions (non-compete, non-solicitation)
  • GDPR-compliant data processing documentation
06

Dismissal & Disciplinary

Before dismissing an employee — even one whose conduct or performance is clearly unsatisfactory — there are legal steps you must follow. Failure to follow a fair process can expose you to an unfair dismissal claim, even if the underlying reason for dismissal was valid. We advise on the correct process and help you manage the situation correctly from the start.

  • Advice on grounds for dismissal
  • Guidance on fair disciplinary and dismissal procedures
  • Performance improvement plans (PIPs)
  • Gross misconduct — investigations and dismissal
  • Defending Employment Tribunal claims
07

Redundancy & Restructuring

Running a fair redundancy process requires careful planning. Whether you are making one redundancy or a collective redundancy of 20 or more employees, there are strict procedural rules that must be followed. We advise employers on how to conduct the process correctly — protecting you from claims while treating your employees fairly.

  • Individual and collective redundancy processes
  • Selection criteria and pool identification
  • Consultation requirements
  • Drafting settlement agreements for departing employees
  • TUPE obligations on business transfers
Act promptly.
Employment claims are time-limited
Time limits

Employment claims have strict deadlines.

Most Employment Tribunal claims must be brought within three months less one day from the date of the act complained of — the dismissal, the discriminatory act, or the last in a series of events. Before you can bring a claim, you must also go through the ACAS Early Conciliation process, which pauses the time limit for a period.

Do not wait. If you believe you may have a claim, contact us as soon as possible so that we can assess your position and advise you on any steps that need to be taken before the time limit expires.
