Why it matters

Without a Will, the law decides for you.

Many people assume that when they die, their estate will automatically pass to their partner or children. Without a valid Will, however, the intestacy rules apply — and the outcome may be very different from what you would have wanted. Unmarried partners have no automatic right to inherit, regardless of how long they have been together.

Making a Will takes less time than most people expect, and provides certainty and peace of mind for you and those you love. We guide you through the decisions simply and without jargon.

  • Straightforward and affordable — most Wills completed at one appointment
  • Advice on inheritance tax planning
  • Protecting children's interests, especially in blended families
  • Safe custody of your Will and documents
What we handle

Planning ahead and looking after those left behind.

01

Making a Will

We take the time to understand your family, your assets and your wishes before preparing a Will that properly reflects them. Whether your circumstances are straightforward or complex — second marriages, children from different relationships, business assets, overseas property — we ensure your Will is legally valid and effective.

02

Updating an Existing Will

Your circumstances change over time — marriage, divorce, new children, new assets, a change in your wishes. A Will should be reviewed regularly, particularly after a major life event. Marriage automatically revokes a previous Will in most circumstances. We advise on when and how to update your existing arrangements, whether by a simple codicil or a new Will.

  • Review of existing Wills
  • Codicils (amendments to existing Wills)
  • New Wills to replace outdated ones
  • Post-death variations (Deeds of Variation)
03

Lasting Powers of Attorney

A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) appoints someone you trust to make decisions on your behalf if you lose mental or physical capacity — through illness, an accident or in later life. There are two types: one covering property and financial affairs, and one covering health and welfare decisions. We strongly recommend making both while you have capacity to do so.

An LPA can only be made by someone who has capacity at the time. Without one, your family may face a costly and time-consuming application to the Court of Protection to manage your affairs. We prepare both types of LPA and manage the registration process with the Office of the Public Guardian.

  • Property & Financial Affairs LPA
  • Health & Welfare LPA
  • LPAs for individuals and for business owners
  • Registration with the Office of the Public Guardian
  • Advice for attorneys on their duties
04

Probate & Estate Administration

When someone dies, their estate usually needs to go through a legal process before assets can be distributed to the beneficiaries. If there is a Will, we apply for a Grant of Probate; if there is no Will, we apply for Letters of Administration. We handle the entire process on behalf of the executors or administrators, providing regular updates and clear accounts throughout.

  • Applying for Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration
  • Collecting and valuing all assets
  • Settling debts, liabilities and expenses
  • Dealing with HMRC — income tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax
  • Selling or transferring property
  • Distributing the estate and preparing final estate accounts
  • Advising beneficiaries on their rights

See our costs page for typical estate administration fees.

05

Court of Protection & Deputyship

Where a person has lost mental capacity and has no LPA in place, a family member or other person close to them may apply to the Court of Protection to be appointed as a deputy. This allows them to manage the person's financial affairs, and in some cases their health and welfare decisions. Deputyship applications are more time-consuming and expensive than an LPA — which is why we always recommend making an LPA in advance.

  • Property & Financial Affairs deputyship applications
  • Health & Welfare deputyship applications (where required)
  • Advice to deputies on their ongoing duties and reporting obligations
  • Applications to the Court of Protection for one-off decisions
What to expect

Making a Will — simply explained.

01

Initial appointment

We meet with you — in person at any of our offices, or by telephone if you prefer — to understand your wishes, your family structure and your assets. We will explain the options available to you in plain English.

02

Draft Will prepared

We prepare a draft Will that reflects everything discussed. We send it to you for review and answer any questions. For most straightforward Wills, this takes 1–2 weeks.

03

Execution

The Will must be signed in front of two independent witnesses. We can assist with this at our offices. The execution must follow specific legal formalities — we ensure yours is valid.

04

Safe storage

We recommend storing the original Will securely. We can hold it in our deeds storage, and ensure your executors know where it is when the time comes.
